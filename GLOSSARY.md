# SEO Glossary (plain-English)

Use this when explaining playbooks to beginners, or when a playbook uses a term or metric name your tools call something else. Terms are grouped by topic; each has a one-line definition and, where useful, the equivalent names different tools use.

## Search basics
- **SEO (search engine optimization)**: improving a site so it earns more unpaid (organic) traffic from search engines.
- **SERP**: search engine results page, the page you see after searching.
- **Organic vs paid**: organic results are earned; paid results are ads (PPC).
- **SERP features**: anything that isn't a plain blue link: featured snippets, People Also Ask (PAA), image packs, video carousels, local pack, AI Overviews.
- **Featured snippet**: the boxed answer at the top of some results, pulled from one page.
- **People Also Ask (PAA)**: the expandable box of related questions.
- **Zero-click search**: a search that ends without a click because the SERP already answered it.
- **Crawling / indexing / ranking**: the bot discovers a page (crawl), stores it (index), then orders it against other pages for a query (rank).
- **Algorithm update**: a change Google makes to its ranking systems. "Core updates" are broad ones that happen a few times a year.
- **E-E-A-T**: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust. Google's quality-rater framework for judging whether content is credible.
- **Helpful content**: Google's push to reward content written for people rather than for rankings.

## Keywords
- **Keyword / query**: the words someone types into a search engine.
- **Seed keyword**: a broad starting term you expand into a list.
- **Search volume**: estimated monthly searches for a keyword.
- **Traffic potential (TP)**: estimated traffic the #1 page gets across *all* the keywords it ranks for. Usually a better sizing number than volume.
- **Parent topic**: the broader keyword that a top-ranking page gets most of its traffic from. If two keywords share a parent topic, target them with one page.
- **Keyword difficulty (KD)**: a 0–100 estimate of how hard a keyword is to rank for, usually based on links. Every tool calculates it differently.
- **Long-tail keyword**: a specific, low-volume query, often 3+ words. Easier to rank for.
- **Search intent**: why someone searches. The four types are informational, navigational, commercial and transactional.
- **Three Cs**: content **type** (blog, product, category), **format** (list, how-to, review) and **angle** (the hook, like "for beginners"). Copy all three from the top results.
- **Keyword cannibalization**: several of your pages competing for the same keyword, which splits signals between them.
- **Keyword mapping**: assigning each keyword or cluster to exactly one URL.
- **Keyword clustering**: grouping keywords that the same page can rank for, usually because their SERPs overlap.
- **Clicks per search (CPS)**: average clicks a keyword produces per search. Below 1 means many searches end without a click.
- **SERP similarity / overlap**: how many of the same URLs rank for two keywords. High overlap means one page can target both.
- **SERP cracks**: weak spots in a top 10, like thin, outdated, forum or low-authority pages, that suggest a keyword is beatable.
- **Personal (custom) KD**: a site-specific difficulty score that blends third-party KD with your own topical relevance and authority. The playbooks give one example weighting; tune it to your site.
- **Business potential**: how easily you can pitch your product inside content about a keyword (scored 0–3).

## Content and on-page
- **Title tag**: the `<title>` in the HTML `<head>`, shown as the clickable headline in results.
- **Meta description**: the HTML summary sometimes shown under the title. Not a ranking factor, but it affects clicks.
- **H1/H2**: on-page headings. Use one H1 per page; H2s are subtopics.
- **Slug**: the end part of a URL, like `/keyword-research/`.
- **Alt text**: a text description of an image, used for accessibility and image search.
- **Schema / structured data**: code (usually JSON-LD) that labels content for search engines and can unlock rich results.
- **Rich results / rich snippets**: enhanced listings with stars, prices, FAQs and similar.
- **Topic cluster / pillar page**: a broad hub page linked to detailed subpages on related subtopics.
- **Topical authority**: being seen as a deep source on a subject, because you cover it thoroughly.
- **Information gain**: the new information your page adds that other ranking pages don't have.
- **Content decay**: traffic slowly dropping as a page ages or competitors improve.
- **Content pruning**: improving, merging, redirecting or deleting weak pages.
- **Thin content**: pages with little unique value.
- **ABC formula (titles)**: Adjective + Benefit + Confidence booster, for example "Proven Tips to Sleep Better (Tested 2026)".
- **Topical map**: the full list of topics and subtopics a site should cover to own a niche, grouped into clusters.
- **Entity / entity density**: entities are things search engines and AI recognize (people, brands, products, concepts). Entity density is how many relevant ones a passage names explicitly.
- **TOFU / MOFU / BOFU**: top, middle and bottom of the funnel. These are content for awareness, consideration and purchase.
- **YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)**: topics that can affect health, finances, safety or legal outcomes. Google holds them to higher E-E-A-T standards.
- **srcset**: an HTML attribute that serves different image sizes to different screens.
- **Programmatic SEO**: generating many pages from a template plus a dataset.

## Links and authority
- **Backlink / inbound link**: a link from another site to yours.
- **Referring domains (RDs)**: the number of unique sites linking to you. This usually matters more than the total link count.
- **Internal link**: a link from one page on your site to another page on the same site.
- **Anchor text**: the clickable words of a link.
- **Dofollow / nofollow / sponsored / UGC**: link attributes. Nofollow, sponsored and UGC hint that the link shouldn't pass ranking credit.
- **PageRank / link equity / "link juice"**: the ranking value that passes through links.
- **Domain authority score (DR / DA / AS / TF)**: a third-party 0–100 score of a site's link strength. Tools use different names. Google doesn't use any of them.
- **Page-level authority (UR / PA)**: the same idea measured for a single URL.
- **Link intersect / common backlinks report**: shows sites that link to several competitors but not to you. These are your easiest prospects.
- **Linkable asset**: content that attracts links naturally, like original data, tools or guides.
- **Digital PR**: earning press coverage and links with newsworthy stories or data.
- **Skyscraper technique**: creating a better version of a heavily linked page, then pitching the sites that link to the original.
- **Broken link building**: finding dead pages that still have links pointing at them, then offering your page as the replacement.
- **Unlinked mention**: a site names your brand but doesn't link to you.
- **PBN (private blog network)**: a network of sites built only to link to a money site. It violates Google's spam policies.
- **Niche edit**: a link inserted into an existing article, usually paid. It is treated as a link scheme when paid.
- **Middleman technique**: send links to a linkable page that links internally to your money page, which is harder to get links to directly.
- **FOI request**: a freedom-of-information request for public data, used as a source for digital PR stories.
- **CTLD (country-code TLD, ccTLD)**: a country domain such as `.de` or `.co.uk`.
- **Toxic / spammy links**: manipulative links. Google mostly ignores them; disavow only if you have a manual action.
- **Disavow**: a file telling Google to ignore specific links.

## Technical
- **Robots.txt**: a file that tells crawlers which paths not to crawl. It doesn't stop indexing.
- **Meta robots / noindex**: a tag that tells search engines not to index a page.
- **Canonical tag**: tells search engines which URL is the preferred version of duplicate or near-duplicate pages.
- **301 / 302 redirect**: a 301 is permanent and passes signals; a 302 is temporary.
- **Redirect chain**: A→B→C. Collapse it to A→C.
- **XML sitemap**: a list of URLs you want indexed.
- **Crawl budget**: how much of your site Googlebot will crawl. It only matters for very large sites.
- **Orphan page**: a page with no internal links pointing to it.
- **Core Web Vitals**: Google's page experience metrics: **LCP** (loading, under 2.5s), **INP** (responsiveness, under 200ms; it replaced FID in March 2024) and **CLS** (visual stability, under 0.1).
- **Mobile-first indexing**: Google ranks you based on your mobile version.
- **Hreflang**: tags that point each language or region to the right version of a page.
- **JavaScript SEO**: making sure content rendered with JavaScript can be crawled and indexed.
- **Faceted navigation**: filter URLs (like `?color=red`) that can create crawl traps.
- **Log file analysis**: reading server logs to see what bots actually crawl.
- **TTFB (time to first byte)**: how fast the server starts responding.
- **HSTS**: a header that forces browsers to use HTTPS.
- **X-Robots-Tag**: an HTTP-header version of meta robots that works for PDFs and other non-HTML files.
- **Googlebot verification**: confirm real Googlebot by reverse DNS lookup or Google's published IP ranges, not by user-agent alone.
- **Index coverage statuses**: Search Console states such as "Crawled – currently not indexed" and "Discovered – currently not indexed". Each one has its own playbook.

## Local
- **Google Business Profile (GBP)**: your free business listing on Google Maps and in local search. It used to be called Google My Business.
- **Local pack / map pack**: the map with three businesses shown for local searches.
- **NAP**: Name, Address, Phone. Keep it identical everywhere it appears.
- **Citation**: any online mention of your NAP, such as a directory listing.
- **SAB (service-area business)**: a business that serves customers at their location, like a plumber. Hide the address in Google Business Profile if customers don't visit.
- **Barnacle SEO**: getting your business listed on pages that already rank, like directories and "best X in city" lists.
- **Location page**: a unique page for each physical location or service area.

## AI search (AEO/GEO)
- **AEO (answer engine optimization) / GEO (generative engine optimization) / LLMO**: optimizing to be cited or mentioned in AI answers. The names are used almost interchangeably.
- **AI Overviews (AIO)**: Google's AI-generated summary at the top of some results.
- **AI Mode**: Google's conversational, chat-style search mode.
- **LLM**: large language model, the engine behind AI assistants.
- **RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)**: the AI searches the web or an index, then writes an answer from what it retrieved.
- **Query fan-out**: the AI splits one prompt into many sub-queries and searches each one.
- **Citation vs mention**: a citation is a linked source in the answer; a mention is your brand named in the answer text.
- **AI visibility / share of voice**: how often your brand appears in AI answers for your topic, compared with competitors.
- **Chunk optimization**: writing sections that each make sense on their own, so AI can lift them cleanly.
- **BLUF**: Bottom Line Up Front. Answer first, then explain.
- **AI crawlers (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended…)**: the user agents AI companies use. Check that robots.txt and your CDN/WAF don't block the ones you want.
- **Reciprocal rank fusion**: a way to merge rankings from several sub-queries (fan-out) into one list. Pages that rank across many sub-queries win.
- **Content engineering**: structuring content, data and templates so both people and machines (search, AI) can parse and reuse it.
- **llms.txt**: a proposed file that lists your key pages for AI systems. Adoption and impact so far are minimal.

## Metrics and measurement
- **Impressions / clicks / CTR / average position**: the core Search Console metrics. CTR = clicks ÷ impressions.
- **Organic traffic**: visits from unpaid search results.
- **Share of search**: your brand's share of branded searches in your category.
- **Bounce rate / engagement rate**: the share of visits that leave without interacting (the inverse of engagement).
- **Pogo-sticking**: a user clicks a result, quickly returns to the SERP and clicks another one.
- **Conversion**: a desired action, like a signup, sale or lead.
- **SEO forecasting**: projecting traffic or revenue from target rankings, using volume × CTR curve × conversion rate.

## Business and reporting
- **KPI / OKR**: a key performance indicator; Objectives and Key Results, a goal-setting framework that cascades from company to team to individual.
- **MQL / SQL**: marketing-qualified lead and sales-qualified lead.
- **Traffic value**: what your organic traffic would cost as ads (clicks × CPC).
- **SoV / SoTV**: share of voice (your share of visibility for a keyword set) and share of traffic value (the same, weighted by traffic value). Executives respond better to SoTV.
- **SEO maturity model**: stages from ad-hoc to integrated SEO inside an organization. It's used to choose the right first moves.

## Tool categories
The playbooks never name a tool vendor. When an article says one of the following, use whatever the user has. Free options are listed first.

| Playbook says | What it means | Free starting point |
|---|---|---|
| search console | Google's own performance and indexing data for your site | Google Search Console |
| a keyword research tool | Search volume, keyword difficulty, related keywords, SERP overview | Search Console queries, Google autocomplete, People Also Ask, Google Keyword Planner |
| a backlink analysis tool | Backlinks and referring domains for any site | Search Console "Links" report (your own site only) |
| a site audit crawler | Crawls your site to find technical issues | Free tiers of desktop crawlers, Search Console "Pages" report |
| a rank tracking tool | Tracks positions over time | Search Console average position |
| a speed / page experience tester | Measures Core Web Vitals | PageSpeed Insights, Lighthouse in Chrome DevTools |
| an AI visibility tracker | Monitors brand mentions in AI answers | Manually test prompts in each AI assistant and log the results in a spreadsheet |
| web analytics | Sessions, conversions, referrers (including AI referrers) | Google Analytics 4 |
| Google search operators | `site:`, `intitle:`, `inurl:`, `"exact"`, `-exclude` | Free, typed into Google |

## Report names you may see in playbooks
Some playbooks use common report names from SEO suites. Every major suite has an equivalent, and most have a free manual alternative.

| Name in playbook | What it does | Manual or free alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Matching terms / Related terms / Also rank for / Also talk about | Expands a seed into keywords that contain it, or keywords top pages also rank for | Google autocomplete, People Also Ask, related searches, Search Console queries |
| Questions report / Questions tab | Question-form keywords for a topic | PAA boxes, autocomplete with who/what/how prefixes, forums |
| Parent topic / Clusters by parent topic | Groups keywords that the same #1 page ranks for | Google both keywords. If 4+ of the top 10 URLs overlap, use one page |
| Content gap / Keyword gap / Compare with | Keywords competitors rank for and you don't | Search competitors' sitemaps and top pages, then check your coverage manually |
| Link intersect / Common backlinks | Sites linking to several competitors but not you | Your own spreadsheet merge of competitor backlink exports |
| Top pages | A site's pages ranked by estimated organic traffic | For your own site: Search Console → Pages sorted by clicks |
| Site structure | Traffic and links aggregated by folder | Group Search Console pages by URL folder in a spreadsheet |
| Organic keywords | Keywords a URL or site ranks for | Search Console → Queries (your site only) |
| Batch / bulk analysis | Metrics for a list of many URLs at once | Paste URLs into your tool's bulk mode, or use its API |
| Portfolios | Saved groups of URLs or competitors you track together | A spreadsheet of tracked URLs |
| Cited domains / AI responses / Mentions gap / Others only | AI visibility views: which domains AI cites, raw answers, prompts where competitors are mentioned and you aren't | Manual prompt testing log (see the ai-search skill's manual AI visibility audit playbook) |
| Opportunities report | Auto-detected quick wins (internal links, snippets, low-CTR pages) | Search Console: position 4–15 queries with low CTR |
| Exact URL / Domain / Prefix mode | Whether a backlink or keyword report covers one URL, a whole domain or a folder | Same concept in any tool |
